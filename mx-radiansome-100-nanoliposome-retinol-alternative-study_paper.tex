\begin{abstract}
Background: The Mexican skincare market exhibits high sensitivity to traditional retinoids, driving demand for gentle, botanical-based anti-aging alternatives. This study evaluates Radiansome™ 100, a microfluidizer-crafted nanoliposome delivery system utilizing water- and oil-soluble actives within skin-identical phospholipid shells. Methods: A stratified survey of 200 consumers across Guadalajara, Monterrey, and Mexico City assessed texture, absorption, efficacy perception, and regulatory trust. Quantitative Results: The composite mean score was 3.68 (Top-2-Box 62.7\%). Absorption speed received the highest rating (Mean 4.2, Top-2-Box 96.0\%), validating its suitability for morning routines before sunscreen application. Confidence in anti-aging efficacy via liposomal delivery scored 4.18 (Top-2-Box 90.5\%). However, trust in COFEPRIS regulatory adherence remained low (Mean 2.83, Top-2-Box 2.5\%), indicating a significant communication gap. Strategic Implications: While 100\% of respondents cited skin irritation as the primary driver for switching from retinol, barriers such as high price (96.0\%) and brand recognition (84.0\%) persist. The Net Promoter Score (NPS) of +8.5 suggests moderate advocacy potential, contingent upon addressing regulatory transparency and supply chain availability.

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Radiansome 100, Microfluidizer Technology, Nanoliposome Delivery, Retinol Alternative, COFEPRIS Compliance, Skin Barrier Repair
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The core innovation of Radiansome™ 100 lies in its microfluidizer-crafted nanoliposome structure, designed to encapsulate both water- and oil-soluble active ingredients within phospholipid shells that mimic the skin’s natural lipid matrix. This formulation targets the stratum corneum penetration barrier, offering a 'targeted release' mechanism intended to enhance bioavailability without the irritation associated with synthetic retinoids. The study methodology involved a stratified sample of 200 participants distributed across three major Mexican metropolitan areas: Guadalajara (temperate highland), Monterrey (semi-arid), and Mexico City (high-altitude dry). Participants were segmented by skin type, income tier, occupation (B2B vs. C-End), and MBTI personality profiles to capture nuanced behavioral data. The survey instrument utilized Likert-scale questions for perceptual metrics and multiple-choice items for usage history and competitive benchmarking.
\section{2. Quantitative Performance \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Quantitative analysis reveals strong performance in sensory and functional attributes. The absorption speed of the nanoliposome system achieved a mean score of 4.2 with a Top-2-Box percentage of 96.0\%, indicating near-universal acceptance for use in morning routines prior to sunscreen application. The lightweight texture was rated positively (Mean 3.67, Top-2-Box 66.5\%), suggesting adequate adaptation to humid conditions in regions like Guadalajara. Moisture retention without greasy residue scored 3.61 (Top-2-Box 63.5\%), appealing to combination skin types. However, perceived value for money lagged slightly (Mean 3.58, Top-2-Box 55.5\%), reflecting price sensitivity in the middle-income segment. Microclimate-specific data showed no significant deviation in overall satisfaction across the three cities, though users in Mexico City reported slightly lower scores for texture lightness compared to those in higher humidity zones.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Demographic stratification highlights distinct adoption drivers. Income level correlated strongly with satisfaction; Affluent/High Net Worth individuals scored highest (Mean 3.9), while Lower-Middle Income segments scored lowest (Mean 3.49). Occupationally, B2B aesthetic clinic owners exhibited the highest engagement (Mean 3.89), likely due to professional familiarity with advanced delivery systems. In contrast, cross-border e-commerce sellers showed lower confidence (Mean 3.52), possibly linked to supply chain uncertainties. MBTI analysis revealed that intuitive thinkers (INTP, ENTJ) valued the mechanism of action, scoring higher on 'targeted release' trust, whereas feeling types (INFP, ISFJ) prioritized sensory comfort and ethical sourcing. Notably, 100\% of respondents identified 'skin irritation/sensitivity' as the sole reason for seeking a non-retinol substitute, underscoring the critical role of gentleness in product positioning.
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Expansion Strategy}
A critical finding is the low trust in regulatory compliance, with only 2.5\% of respondents expressing confidence in strict adherence to COFEPRIS cosmetic claim regulations (Mean 2.83). This represents a strategic vulnerability. To mitigate this, regional expansion must prioritize transparent clinical documentation and localized educational campaigns regarding safety standards. Barriers to adoption included high price (96.0\%), brand recognition (84.0\%), and supply availability (71.5\%). Conversely, key decision factors for switching were enhanced penetration (100\%), barrier repair (100\%), and reduced inflammation (89.5\%). The Net Promoter Score (NPS) of +8.5 indicates a neutral-to-positive sentiment, but growth will depend on resolving the 'trust deficit' through visible compliance certifications and leveraging the strong preference for peptide serum benchmarks (79.0\%) over prescription retinoids.

\section{Conclusion}
The evaluation of Radiansome™ 100 in the Mexican market demonstrates robust consumer acceptance of its microfluidizer-crafted nanoliposome technology, particularly regarding absorption speed and non-irritating efficacy. With a composite mean of 3.68 and a Top-2-Box rate of 62.7\%, the product successfully addresses the primary pain point of retinol-induced sensitivity. However, significant challenges remain in regulatory trust (COFEPRIS adherence score of 2.83) and price perception. Strategic recommendations include enhancing transparency around clinical validation to bridge the trust gap, optimizing distribution channels to address supply concerns, and targeting B2B aesthetic professionals who show higher receptivity. By reinforcing the narrative of 'botanical patent anti-aging' and leveraging the high intent for barrier repair, RIMAN can convert moderate advocacy into sustained market leadership in Latin America.
